## Query
How does infill pattern and fiber orientation affect the compressive strength and energy absorption of 3D printed continuous fiber-reinforced composite lattice structures?

## Sage Synthesis
The interplay between infill pattern and fiber orientation represents a hierarchy of structural control, where macro-scale geometry dictates load paths and micro-scale alignment determines resistance to those loads. Macroscopically, the infill pattern (e.g., Octet vs. grid) establishes the deformation mechanism: stretch-dominated geometries channel forces axially for higher stiffness and strength, while bending-dominated geometries rely on flexure. To realize the potential of stretch-dominated patterns, fiber orientation must be optimized; axial alignment significantly increases the critical buckling load of individual struts. Conversely, misalignment creates resin-rich areas that lead to premature shear failure. For energy absorption, the synthesis requires geometries that facilitate progressive, layer-by-layer collapse rather than catastrophic shear banding, combined with fiber orientations that provide sufficient toughness to maintain a long, stable plateau stress.

## Analogies
- **Architecture**: Load Path Alignment → Infill patterns function like the skeletal geometry of a Gothic cathedral (e.g., flying buttresses vs. rib vaults), determining whether loads are channeled primarily through compression or tension. Fiber orientation acts like the placement of iron tie-rods within the stone masonry; just as rods must align with the curve of an arch to effectively resist tension, fibers must align with the strut axis to prevent buckling and utilize the material's full tensile capacity.

## Ethical Dimensions
- Resource Stewardship: Optimizing the synergy between infill and fiber alignment allows for maximum strength with minimal material, aligning with principles of sustainable engineering and reducing waste in additive manufacturing.
- Verification and Safety: As lattice structures become more complex to optimize performance, the difficulty of non-destructive testing and verification increases. Engineers bear an ethical responsibility to ensure that the internal complexity does not introduce unforeseen failure modes in critical applications.

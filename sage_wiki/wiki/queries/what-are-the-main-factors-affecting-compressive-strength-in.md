## Query
What are the main factors affecting compressive strength in 3D printed fiber-reinforced composite structures?

## Sage Synthesis
The compressive strength of 3D printed fiber-reinforced composites is governed by a complex interplay of process-induced defects and material properties. The most significant factor is porosity, as the layer-by-layer deposition inherently creates microscopic gaps between raster roads and layers; these voids act as stress concentrators that drastically reduce the effective load-bearing area. Closely related is interlayer bonding strength, which is often the 'weakest link' in the Z-axis; insufficient thermal energy during deposition leads to poor polymer diffusion across layers, causing delamination under load. Additionally, fiber orientation plays a critical role: fibers aligned with the loading direction effectively resist buckling, whereas the shear forces of the nozzle can misalign fibers, reducing their reinforcing efficacy. Finally, the fiber-matrix interface determines the efficiency of stress transfer; if the adhesion is poor, the matrix cannot transfer load to the fibers, leading to premature failure.

## Analogies
- **Civil Engineering**: Structural Masonry → One can view a 3D printed composite part like a brick wall. The deposited roads of material are the bricks, and the thermal fusion between layers is the mortar. In masonry, the compressive strength of the wall relies heavily on the mortar quality and the absence of gaps; if the mortar is weak or missing (representing voids or poor interlayer bonding), the wall crumbles under the weight of a roof. The reinforcing fibers act like the steel rebar embedded within the wall, providing internal support that prevents the bricks (the matrix) from buckling inward under the load.

## Ethical Dimensions
- Engineers have an ethical obligation to account for the inherent anisotropy and defect susceptibility of 3D printed parts. Overestimating compressive strength based on idealized material properties rather than actual print quality could lead to catastrophic structural failures in safety-critical applications.
- The pursuit of lightweight, high-strength structures must not compromise the rigorous testing standards required to validate the consistency of interlayer bonding, which is highly variable compared to traditional manufacturing.
